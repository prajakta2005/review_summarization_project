# Novelty Statement

Report-ready text for the Introduction (contributions) and Conclusion. Every number below
comes from `docs/RESULTS.md` (generated by `python experiments/run_all.py`): 40,412 reviews,
10 Amazon categories, real genuine (`OR`) / computer-generated fake (`CG`) labels.

## In one paragraph

Fake-review detection, explainable machine learning and review summarization are each well
studied on their own. This system **couples** them. A per-review credibility score decides how
much each review's sentences count in the product summary. Every summary sentence can be traced
back to its source review, that review's score, and the SHAP explanation of the score.
Low-credibility reviews are surfaced separately instead of being silently deleted. We also
**measure** what this coupling buys. On the full dataset, credibility-aware summaries take
**42%** of their sentences from fake reviews, compared with **92%** for an otherwise identical
summarizer that trusts every review equally (5-sentence summaries, averaged over 10 categories).

## Why an ordinary summarizer is so contaminated

Computer-generated reviews use generic, typical wording. An extractive summarizer that rewards
sentences close to the "centre" of all reviews (TF-IDF centroid) therefore prefers them: 92% of
its sentences came from fake reviews. Weighting by credibility counteracts exactly this bias.

## Limitations (state these in the report)

- In this dataset `product_id` is the Amazon category, so "product" summaries are category-level.
- "Fake" means computer-generated (`CG`). Human-written deceptive reviews may behave differently.
- Summaries are extractive: traceable and never hallucinated, but less fluent than abstractive ones.
- The deployed 6-feature model gives up about 0.19 F1 against the best text model in exchange for explanations a person can follow.
- ROUGE against a genuine-only extractive summary is a proxy for quality, not human judgement.
- Even with weighting, 42% of summary sentences still come from fake reviews. A hard filter at score 60 lowers that to 20%, but ROUGE-L drops (0.405 → 0.315). Better credibility models (see Model Comparison) would reduce it further.
